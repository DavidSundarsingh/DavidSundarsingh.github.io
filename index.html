I am a Ph.D. student at Washington University in St. Louis, where I am advised by Dr. Yiannis Kantaros in the Department of Electrical and Systems Engineering. My research focuses on developing provably safe planning and task interpretation frameworks for uncertain systems and environments, applying Conformal Prediction for rigorous uncertainty quantification for AI-enabled perception systems and foundation models. Prior to my doctoral studies, I earned my Master’s degree in Robotics and Automation from the Defence Institute of Advanced Technology (DIAT), Pune in 2023, and my Bachelor’s degree in Mechanical Engineering from the Coimbatore Institute of Technology (CIT), Coimbatore in 2020.

Projects

  1. Safe Planning in Unknown Environments Using Conformalized Semantic Maps

    This paper proposes a semantic reach-avoid planning framework for unknown environments under perceptual uncertainty without requiring prior assumptions on sensor models or noise distributions. By applying conformal prediction to multi-class semantic maps generated on the fly, the method constructs calibrated prediction sets of semantic labels guaranteed to contain the ground truth with user-defined confidence. A downstream planner adaptively balances exploitation and active exploration over these conformalized maps, consistently outperforming uncertainty-agnostic and heuristically calibrated baselines in mission completion rates.

  2. ConformalNL2LTL: Translating Natural Language Instructions into Temporal Logic Formulas with Conformal Correctness Guarantees

    This paper introduces ConformalNL2LTL, the first framework capable of translating natural language instructions into Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications with user-defined correctness guarantees. The translation process is framed as a sequential question-answering workflow solved collaboratively by a primary and an auxiliary large language model (LLM), with conformal prediction used to calibrate uncertainty and flag when to request auxiliary model or human assistance. Theoretical and empirical evaluations show that the framework reliably achieves target success rates (e.g., up to 99%) on unseen commands while drastically minimizing human intervention.

  3. Safe Multi-Robot Navigation under Uncertainty with User-Specified Risk Budgets Ongoing work

    This paper introduces an iterative, two-stage planning framework that guarantees multi-agent safe navigation under stochastic system disturbances while satisfying an explicit, user-defined global risk budget. The proposed approach decomposes joint chance constraints into individual obstacle, boundary, and inter-agent components, using collision risk as a merit metric to dynamically redistribute risk across robots and time steps. Extensive simulations and real-world multi-drone experiments demonstrate that the planner adaptively adjusts trajectory conservatism to match disturbance levels and reliably keeps empirical collision risk below the specified threshold.
